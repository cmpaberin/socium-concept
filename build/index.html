What we would do first

One workflow, four to six weeks, paid, with a number at the end.

Grace wrote to Thomas that this needs validating through your actual use cases rather than decided on a presentation. We agree, so we are not proposing a platform purchase. We are proposing the smallest piece of work that produces evidence.

  1. Pick one team and one client account. Not the easiest one. The one where reporting hurts most.
  2. We instrument that team's requisition flow. Every stage timestamped, so fill rate and time to fill compute themselves instead of being assembled the night before Job Fillability.
  3. We stand up the live portal for that one client. They log in and see their own pipeline, current. The weekly status email stops.
  4. We measure two things only. Hours per consultant per week returned to actual recruiting, and the change in time to fill on that desk.
  5. You decide with real numbers. If the hours saved do not beat the fee on your own figures, we have both learned something cheaply and you owe us nothing further.

What we would not take on

  • Employee productivity monitoring. Grace mentioned the other proposal focused on managing and monitoring people's productivity. We do not build surveillance tooling. It damages the trust a recruitment business runs on, and in our experience the throughput problem is almost always process, not effort. We will instrument the work. We will not instrument the people.
  • Replacing HubSpot. You have a CRM. Everything above sits alongside it and reads from it. Rip-and-replace is how these projects die.
  • Becoming your applicant tracking system of record in the first engagement. Too much risk in one step. We would integrate, prove the value, and only then discuss consolidation if you wanted it.
  • Touching candidate personal data without a data processing agreement in place and named retention rules. You hold identity documents and salary data across three jurisdictions. That paperwork comes before the build, not after.
  • Quoting a number today. Effort bands above are honest ranges. Commercial terms come from Tony Hilden, our founder, once we know which workflow we are starting with. We would rather scope one thing properly than price a wishlist.
